# Cat to Postcard — English Prompts

Upload a photograph of your own cat. For styles 01–09 and 11–14, paste the common block followed by one numbered style block. Style 10 is a shorter standalone prompt. Replace all placeholders with observed details and your own short title/subtitle before generation.

## Common reference and output block

```text
SUBJECT AND REFERENCE ROLES
Create one finished postcard artwork of [CAT NAME OR “THE CAT”], the same individual real cat in the supplied photograph. The photograph is the sole authority for the cat's face geometry, ear angles, muzzle, eye and nose colors, visible body proportions, pose, life stage, and exact asymmetric coat markings. If more than one photo of the same cat is supplied, Photo 1 controls face identity, Photo 2 controls body and coat map, and Photo 3 controls expression or pose. Never blend cats or mirror a marking. If the source crops or hides a body part, preserve that crop or simplify conservatively; do not invent unseen legs, patches or tail.

Observed identity anchors: (1) [true face, ear and muzzle proportions]; (2) [real forehead, cheek, nose and chest marking boundaries]; (3) [visible body shape or characteristic expression/pose]. Preserve the photographed cat's age and expression. Rebuild the artistic medium only; the source background is only context. Replace indoor clutter, plants or outdoor scenery with the selected style's sparse paper, color field or small design accent; imply no particular place or living situation.

OUTPUT AND TYPOGRAPHY
Create a single standalone 3:4 portrait postcard front, viewed straight-on, with a print-safe margin of about 5%. The cat must be legible at postcard size. There is no source-photo comparison panel, product mockup, back-of-card address side or second cat. Use the selected style block to position its art, paper, controlled ornament and two levels of lettering. Replace [TITLE] with one or two short English words and [SUBTITLE] with two to five short English words before generation. Render these exact supplied strings once each, including their supplied letter case, spelled correctly, with clear letterforms; do not print the placeholder brackets. Treat the supplied title and subtitle as a literal text lock: do not substitute a guessed cat name, coat description, medium label or different phrase. A style may explicitly request one additional truthful [SIDE NOTE]; otherwise generate no other words. The style block's lettering construction is a required part of the art medium: do not default every design to the same Roman uppercase serif with a widely spaced uppercase subtitle. Never invent a date, place, school, shelter, owner, coordinate, postal value, edition number, artist signature, brand, logo or watermark. Keep all typography off the cat's identity landmarks. If the exact words cannot be rendered, leave their reserved paper area clean rather than filling it with pseudo-text. The cat's identity takes priority over decoration.
```

## 01 — Oil pastel

```text
STYLE — OIL PASTEL POSTCARD
Create one finished 3:4 vertical postcard front of the real cat in the supplied photo. Draw the cat as a carefully observed oil-pastel subject on warm, toothy ivory paper. Keep the photographed crop, whether a close head portrait, three-quarter pose or full body, and preserve the visible face, ear angles, muzzle, eyes, nose, paws, body mass and coat-patch boundaries. Use short directional wax strokes, broken pigment and broad scumbled layers; resolve the diagnostic facial markings with smaller crisp strokes. Keep naturally light fur luminous with reserved paper and subtle pastel shadows; preserve darker fur at its observed value and never invent a new coat pattern.

COMPOSITION AND SPACE
Place the cat slightly above and to the right of center, occupying about 55–60% of the card height. Add only two short dry-pastel echo strokes in colors sampled from the real cat, behind one side of the silhouette; otherwise leave the background bare. Preserve at least one third of the ivory paper as visibly unmarked breathing room, especially at the lower left. Leave a clean 15% footer zone; the pastel strokes can taper toward it but must never run behind the text. Keep a modest safe margin at all four edges; no ruled postcard back, printed border or floating sticker outline.

TYPOGRAPHY AND SMALL DETAIL
Hand-letter [TITLE] near the lower left in a small, soft humanist mixed-case pencil alphabet: rounded open counters, gently irregular baseline and pressure changes that visibly belong beside oil pastel, while every letter remains clear. Put [SUBTITLE] one line below as much smaller, neat lowercase pencil lettering, not widely tracked uppercase print type. Align both to the same left edge and keep the word shapes compact. Beside the title add only one tiny dry pastel color swatch taken from the real cat; it cannot cross a letter. The title is an artist's restrained note, never a formal serif caption, a curly script or a childish crayon heading.

Avoid glossy gradients, candy colors, a filled landscape, large flowers, overlapping text and fur, oversized headline type, pseudo-lettering, extra captions, a second cat, kitten-like proportions and altered markings.
```

## 02 — Oil painting

```text
STYLE — DECORATIVE OIL PAINTING POSTCARD
Create one finished 3:4 vertical gallery-like postcard front. Paint the real cat with visible bristle strokes, layered opaque paint, dry-brush breaks and limited restrained impasto over subtle linen tooth. Preserve the exact adult head silhouette, ears, muzzle, natural eye shape, pose and true asymmetric coat-color regions before introducing painterly shadow color. Moss, muted blue-grey and dusty violet may tint shadows but may not become false markings. The eyes, nose and muzzle remain sharply identifiable.

COMPOSITION AND SPACE
Concentrate the painted cat and one or two broad abstract brush daubs in an asymmetric right-hand vignette occupying roughly the central 60–65% of the card width. Let the vignette fade with lost-and-found edges into pale, lightly brushed linen; keep a broad quiet strip along the left and a calm lower edge. Preserve about 25–30% of the surface as lightly toned, nearly unpainted linen. No formal rectangular oil-painting frame or reconstructed indoor/outdoor backdrop. Keep abstract brush daubs behind the cat and out of the face.

TYPOGRAPHY AND SMALL DETAIL
Typeset [TITLE] in the quiet upper-left linen as a restrained gallery Didone in mixed case: fine hairlines, decisive verticals and elegant thick-thin contrast, at medium size rather than a tall poster headline. Print [SUBTITLE] just below in a very small narrow book italic, with normal word spacing and no all-capital letterspacing. Keep type flat, dark and sharply printed against the impasto. One thin hand-drawn umber caption rule sits below the subtitle and stops before the cat. This is the single classical editorial serif of the series; do not turn it into the same uppercase Roman title used elsewhere.

Avoid a heavy frame, crowded florals, rainbow face patches, dark dramatic backdrops, text over impasto, generic idealized kitten features, invented highlights that change coat identity, signatures, exhibition dates and other unsupplied metadata.
```

## 03 — Watercolor

```text
STYLE — TRANSPARENT WATERCOLOR POSTCARD
Create one finished 3:4 vertical postcard front on natural ivory cold-press paper. Begin with an accurate observational drawing of the same real cat, then use transparent watercolor glazes. Protect the true shape of the eyes, ear bases, muzzle, nose, mouth and asymmetric diagnostic coat edges. Reserve clean paper only for fur that is truly pale in the reference; use transparent washes matching the real tones everywhere else. Let wet-on-wet blooms, light granulation and capillary edges appear in the body and in only two or three small abstract wash blooms; use crisp dry brush mainly where the face needs clarity.

COMPOSITION AND SPACE
Place the photographed portion of the cat in the lower-right to middle-right area; a close portrait remains a close portrait, while a visible full body may be shown in full. Keep the top-left quadrant and at least 45% of the whole card visibly dry, pale paper. Let the background wash dissolve naturally before it reaches the text area. One faint broken wash arc may echo the cat's pose, but it never becomes a painted rectangular backdrop. Keep a clean paper edge and safe margin all around.

TYPOGRAPHY AND SMALL DETAIL
Write [TITLE] in the upper-left dry-paper area as a small field-journal italic in fine warm-grey pen: gently slanted, observant, airy and unmistakably readable, with open counters and no ornamental loops. Place [SUBTITLE] directly below as minute upright humanist roman lowercase rather than small-cap display type. Keep both in a narrow left column with ample dry paper beneath. A short very pale watercolor underline can sit below the words but must stop before the cat; do not soak pigment through letters. One nearly transparent wash arc near the lower-right balances the text without crossing the cat. Avoid a large formal serif title or illegible flowing calligraphy.

Avoid gouache opacity, black all-over outlines, random splashes over the face, colorful paper flooding, a filled scenery panel, lettering inside wet wash, decorative botanical clutter, altered coat boundaries and additional words.
```

## 04 — Continuous line

```text
STYLE — OBSERVATIONAL CONTINUOUS-LINE POSTCARD
Create one finished 3:4 vertical postcard front on smooth cream drawing paper. The cat drawing must be physically traceable as ONE continuous dark-brown pen path without lifting the pen. Begin at one point on the cat, travel through the actual outer silhouette, ear angles, face, pose and tail, and return through connected interior detours only when needed for a distinctive large marking. Let the line vary gently in pressure and wobble like a human hand. If a feature would require a separate stroke, simplify or omit it. The only permitted detached cat marks are at most three tiny marks total for the two eyes and nose. Lettering is separate from this single cat path. Preserve the cat's observed head-to-body ratio, muzzle, legs, tail direction and visible asymmetric markings without turning it into a generic icon. Indicate dark coat areas by their connected boundary alone; leave their interiors the same bare paper. Never fill a coat patch.

COMPOSITION AND SPACE
Keep the complete cat path in a compact, off-center cluster within the middle 45–55% of the card height, with roughly 70% of the cream surface otherwise open. Do not draw a scene or tonal wash. Continue the same uninterrupted cat path from the tail or back into one very fine horizontal line in the lower third, stopping before the postcard trim. This line is the visual bridge to the text; do not force it through the cat's anatomy or through letters.

TYPOGRAPHY AND SMALL DETAIL
The exact title text is [TITLE]. Letter those words once just above the line's left end in a quiet lowercase monoline alphabet made with the same dark-brown pen weight and humane line wobble as the cat contour. The letters have open counters, rounded joins and ordinary word spacing, not a Roman serif or mechanical geometric font. Put [SUBTITLE] immediately below the line in a much smaller matching monoline lowercase. Keep both on untouched paper and clearly readable. A single tiny matte dot in one authentic cat-coat color ends the free line; it is the only separate ornament. Lettering may have its own necessary strokes; it must not add strokes to the cat drawing or run through it.

The line count is the defining feature. At postcard size the cat should read as a spare single-line contour, with bare paper inside almost everywhere. Absolutely no hatching, crosshatching, fur strokes, parallel contour lines, stippling, patch fills, dark masses, shading, engraving or detailed ink-portrait texture. Avoid mechanically perfect vector curves, thick sticker borders, smiley-cartoon features, random color fields, duplicate limbs, continuous-line tricks that change the pose, dense type and illegible pseudo-text.
```

## 05 — Stained glass

```text
STYLE — JEWEL STAINED-GLASS POSTCARD
Create one finished straight-on 3:4 portrait postcard of the single real cat in the supplied photograph. The photo alone controls the cat's visible face and body crop, pose, ear angles, eye and nose colors, and exact asymmetric coat-patch map. Rebuild the cat as a true leaded stained-glass image, never as a photograph with lines drawn over fur. Construct the cat and a simple background from roughly 120–180 closely fitted, individually cut glass pieces. Use smaller pieces around true facial markings while keeping the eyes, nose and mouth readable. Each glass piece contains essentially ONE hue from edge to edge: each piece representing the cat's coat keeps the observed hue of that exact area, while a background piece may stay blue. Minor brightness variation, tiny bubbles, ripples and sharp glossy reflections may occur within the same hue; no piece contains several different colors, painted hair or photographic texture. Fine continuous charcoal-black lead channels follow every real piece boundary and meet physically. They echo genuine coat-patch boundaries without making a random grid across the eyes. Distinctive coat colors occupy groups of same-color shards in their real positions.

COMPOSITION AND SPACE
Set the single cat within one compact pointed-arch or shallow-gothic glass panel in the upper 60% of the postcard. Use deep cobalt and teal background shards, one small moon sliver or two tiny star-shaped shards at most, only where they do not distract from the cat. No ivy, plants, room, landscape or source-photo scenery. Give the panel a thin coherent dark frame. Preserve broad warm-ivory paper side margins and a clean lower quarter; a subtle colored transmitted-light glimmer may touch only the paper immediately beside the panel. This is a flat printable postcard front, not a window installation, floating sticker or product mockup.

TYPOGRAPHY AND SMALL DETAIL
Center [TITLE] beneath the panel in clear handcrafted Arts-and-Crafts flared capitals, warm charcoal ink, with subtly curved stems and open counters. Center [SUBTITLE] directly below in much smaller simple humanist sans capitals. Both lines must be exact and readable on bare paper. Keep a 5% safe margin. No text inside glass, extra words, pseudo-lettering, dates, logos or watermarks.

If a separate style reference is provided, use it ONLY for small single-hue glossy glass pieces and fitted dark leading. Do not copy any depicted cat, multi-cat arrangement, transparent checkerboard, plants, symbols or words from it. Never alter this cat's coat map or facial identity to match a style reference.
```

## 06 — Cloisonné

```text
STYLE — CONTEMPORARY CLOISONNÉ POSTCARD
Make one finished 3:4 postcard front. Reinterpret the photographed individual cat as a planar cloisonné enamel portrait, not a photograph with gold contours. Preserve the real adult silhouette, visible crop, pose, face geometry, eye color and asymmetric coat map. Reduce the cat to about 20–35 purposeful CLOSED color compartments, with a few smaller cells only for the distinctive blaze, eye and nose. Fine antique-brass wires form the shared borders of these compartments and terminate cleanly. Within each cell use one smooth opaque enamel hue from the real cat, with quiet kiln-fired pooling; no fur strands, photoreal shading, glass translucency or metallic scribbles over a fur texture. Keep every light, dark and colored coat region in its photographed position and close to its real value. The cat must remain recognizable at postcard size.

COMPOSITION AND SPACE
Place the graphic cat within one modest inset deep peacock-blue or blue-black enamel color plate in the upper two-thirds of a matte warm-white postcard. Keep its edges simple and flat, with no simulated jewelry pin, product shadow, raised plaque or gold rim around the whole card. The cat should occupy only part of the blue plate, so the ground reads as calm negative space rather than a busy floral field. Add at most one tiny closed-wire geometric star beside it, or leave the blue ground empty. Preserve the entire lower quarter as quiet uncoated paper, with additional unprinted space along the sides.

TYPOGRAPHY AND SMALL DETAIL
In the lower-left paper field set [TITLE] in a distinct 1930s Art Deco geometric sans alphabet: slender straight uppercase strokes, tall verticals, open counters and careful moderate spacing, printed deep blue-black. Avoid Roman serifs and oversized jewel-brand lettering. Put [SUBTITLE] immediately beneath in tiny monoline geometric capitals printed matte antique-brass ink. Keep both flat, precise and outside the enamel plate; a single very short brass tick may align to the left margin. The blue plate, brass wire and geometric type should feel like one designed postcard, with no extra decoration.

Avoid all-over filigree, invented symmetric markings, inflated enamel-pin outlines, stained-glass translucency, photographic fur with gold lines on top, plastic gloss, green leaf wreaths, dense flowers, generic serif headlines, fake seals, logos, extra captions and ornament covering the eyes or nose.
```

## 07 — Relief print

```text
STYLE — HAND-CARVED RELIEF-PRINT POSTCARD
Create one finished 3:4 vertical postcard front as a two-to-four-color reduction linocut or woodblock print of the real cat. Reduce its accurate adult silhouette, pose, face structure and largest asymmetric markings to bold positive and negative masses. Carve real white blaze and chest areas as exposed warm paper, while a dark key block defines eyes, nose, mouth, paws and structural contours. Show deliberate V- and U-gouge cuts, tapered marks and occasional printing chatter following the body's form, never photographic individual hairs. Keep slight ink misregistration away from the face and diagnostic marking borders.

COMPOSITION AND SPACE
Place the cat print slightly to the left in the central 55–60% of the postcard height. Leave about 35–40% of the warm, unprinted printmaking paper open, especially along the right and lower edges. Use at most two small carved wedge or crescent marks behind the cat; no full dark ink field, circle enclosing the image or dense scenery. Keep a broad 15% lower paper band with clean space for type.

TYPOGRAPHY AND SMALL DETAIL
Print [TITLE] on the lower-left band in a compact hand-cut wood-type slab alphabet: sturdy square stems, angular slab serifs, a few human carving irregularities and dark ink pressed into paper. It must look built from carved printing blocks, not a delicate high-contrast book serif. Put [SUBTITLE] at the lower right in much smaller, plain letterpress sans with ordinary spacing. Keep ample paper between them. One tiny V-gouge mark beside the title echoes the cat print; it is not an edition number. Distress only the outer ink edges, never letter identity or spelling.

Avoid smooth vector outlines, halftone dots, photographic fur, excessive distress, a stamp circle, falsely added tabby stripes, title text placed over the cat, fake artist signatures, edition numbers and unsupplied metadata.
```

## 08 — Risograph

```text
STYLE — RISOGRAPH EDITORIAL POSTCARD
Create one complete 3:4 portrait postcard front on warm, uncoated paper. The real cat occupies a compact image field in the lower-left to center, about 45–55% of the card height, with its observed pose and exact face/coat markings intact. Build the cat with three or four translucent spot-ink stencil layers: a structural plate chosen from the cat's darkest true coat value, a second spot-ink plate chosen from its real midtones, and one restrained pale abstract shape plate. Show porous ink, light halftone and slight overprint; restrict any registration slip to peripheral fur and abstract shapes, never eyes, nose or distinctive patch edges.

Leave the top 18% and rightmost 25–30% mostly bare paper. Print [TITLE] near the upper right in a rounded, wide mid-century booklet grotesk with confident medium-weight sans strokes, open counters and the supplied mixed case. It is a compact one-line title, secondary to the cat and visibly larger than [SUBTITLE], with none of the classic book-serif letter shapes used in other styles. Put [SUBTITLE] directly beneath it in tiny narrow monospaced lowercase, like a small print-shop caption. Both lines are straight, perfectly registered and surrounded by clean paper; spot-ink registration effects belong to the image, not the letters. A single offset warm-ink dot near the foot of the text column repeats an authentic cat-coat accent; it is a graphic dot, not a number or seal.

Use at most two broad abstract stencil shapes behind the cat. Preserve at least 35% visibly unprinted paper. Avoid CMYK photo filtering, random ink colors, all-over halftone, misregistered facial features, invented coat patches, fake publication serials and text-like grain.
```

## 09 — Cut paper

```text
STYLE — CUT-PAPER COLLAGE POSTCARD
Make a single 3:4 portrait postcard front from four to seven clearly separated layers of matte hand-cut card. Construct the entire cat from only five to nine large, contiguous flat-color paper pieces for its silhouette and real coat-map regions; up to three or four additional small cut-paper pieces may define the eyes, nose and mouth. Each piece is one unmodulated paper color with a continuous visible cut edge and a consistent shallow contact shadow, so the overlaps and physical layer order are obvious. Preserve this individual cat's observed ears, muzzle, posture and asymmetric patch boundaries through these few shapes. Use gently irregular scissor edges, not jagged fur-like fringes; do not subdivide any coat region into flecks, feathered strips or tiny hair-shaped pieces. No printed photograph, painted fur, photographic shading or hidden cat image may sit beneath the paper. Set the cat as an off-center cluster in the lower-right two-thirds, reaching roughly 55–65% of card height. The base is continuous warm ivory paper, with a broad, uninterrupted upper-left field and at least 35% total plain stock.

Build [TITLE] from a few large editorial letters cut from one muted dark paper: elegant thick-thin contrast, deliberate open counters, straight readable baselines, visible card edges and very shallow contact shadows. The title should feel integral to the collage, as in the uploaded paper-poster examples, rather than ink stamped in a generic font. Balance it against the cat near the upper-left; it may span roughly 40% of the width but cannot cross the face. Print [SUBTITLE] directly below in tiny clean humanist sans on the base paper. The only crafted accent is one gently folded corner exposing a muted color already used somewhere in the approved paper palette; do not fold over a facial feature. One broad cut-paper arch may sit behind the cat, or keep the base plain; reconstruct no room, garden or scenery.

Avoid a photo/art split, paper confetti, more than nine cat color pieces, micro-cuts, feathered fur edges, fur strand detail, photographic shading, cartoon face, arbitrary color patches, titles covering the cat, busy labels, crumpled-page shadows, torn-paper holes or text cut into illegible shapes.

## 11 — Embroidery

```text

Satin stitch appears only in tiny nose or ear accents, stem stitch only where a structural contour needs it. ## 13 — Vintage letterpress

## 14 — Torn paper

```text
NATURAL PAPER BREAKTHROUGH — One luxurious 3:4 postcard front made from a single large ivory sheet over muted sage backing. The cat appears to push through an accidental rupture in the sheet. The hole must NOT be a neat long boat, vertical slit, oval, or deliberately cut aperture. Instead the tear starts around the cat's visible face and leading paw or shoulder and propagates unevenly: asymmetric branching rips, 3–5 broad paper flaps bending toward the viewer in different directions, broken corners, exposed white paper fibers, variable thickness and soft cast shadows. Some parts of the cat overlap the FRONT of the intact sheet so it feels to be emerging through it. Keep the head and identity fully legible and use the source pose rather than inventing a jumping body. Render the cat itself as a physical flat cut-paper collage of about 10–18 opaque matte shapes: separate head, ears, muzzle, broad body and real coat-patch shapes in their observed colors; each piece one flat color, no photoreal fur or glossy eyes. Let the irregular rupture and flaps follow the cat, not frame it in a symmetric shape. Leave a broad untouched right-side and lower field. Set the exact title [TITLE] on two short lines in a tall, light, condensed literary book face in the right-side blank field, with the exact subtitle [SUBTITLE] beneath in tiny book roman. No extra words, leaves, second tear or photo printed under the paper.
```
